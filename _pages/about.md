I am a Research Associate at the Yusuf Hamied Department of Chemistry, University of Cambridge, working with Professor Alex Archibald. In October 2024, I was elected to a Postdoctoral Research Associate position at Pembroke College.

My current research improves the representation of the global marine sulfur cycle in climate models, using the <a href="https://www.ukca.ac.uk/wiki/index.php/UKCA">UK Chemistry and Aerosols model (UKCA)</a> and the <a href="https://ukesm.ac.uk/">UK Earth System Model (UKESM)</a>. Prior to Cambridge, I built over four years of experience in global and regional atmospheric modelling with the <a href="https://emep.int/mscw/"> European Monitoring and Evaluation Programme (EMEP) atmospheric chemistry transport model</a> driven by WRF or ECMWF meteorology. This spans the full modelling workflow — model development, large-ensemble simulation, evaluation against observations, and translating output into policy-relevant assessment.

I have published 10 peer-reviewed papers in high-impact journals, including four as first author from my PhD at the University of Edinburgh and my post at the Norwegian Meteorological Institute (MET Norway), and six as co-author resulting from my MPhil and subsequent postdoctoral work. Full details are available on my Publication page.

I believe the most effective route to understanding Earth's atmosphere is to combine state-of-the-art models with state-of-the-art measurements. My long-term goal is to bring both to bear on questions that matter beyond the literature — delivering evidence and tools that support practical solutions to air pollution and climate change, whether in academia, national forecasting services, or industry.
